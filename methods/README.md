## Data contract and chronology

Let $m$ be a material, $c$ a supplier country, $o$ the latest released
week, $t$ a target week, and $h=t-o$. Each submission row contains
disruption probability $p_{mt}$, benchmark price forecast
$\widehat P_{mt}$, order quantity $Q_{mt}$, and country shares
$s_{mct}$. The required constraints are

$$
0\le p_{mt}\le1,\quad Q_{mt}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad s_{mct}\ge0,\quad
\sum_{c=1}^{6}s_{mct}=1,\quad
s_{mct}=0\text{ if }c\text{ does not supply }m.
$$

There are exactly $10\times26=260$ material/week rows for weeks 209–234.
Known demand extends through 234. The timed inputs—macro, news, outcomes,
prices, shipping, supply history, and tariffs—stop at $o=208$ for Phase 1
and $o=221$ for Phase 2. The
[`Data.until(o)` loader](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/data.py) enforces
this cutoff in historical tests while retaining future demand and the static
supplier catalog. Frozen Phase 1 data lives in
[`data/snapshots/w208`](../data/snapshots/w208); Phase 2 data is
stored directly in [`data/`](../data), with `data/Phase 2 data/` a
compatibility symlink for frozen generators. Public 209–221 truth became
available only in Phase 2 and is used for retrospective public optimization;
private 222–234 outcomes remain unobserved.
For reviewed teams, final placement combines 80% private score and 20%
judging; up to two CSVs can be selected. The required methodology video is
roughly three minutes. These submission rules make private-window quality
the relevant decision criterion, while released public rows help calibrate
the local scorer.

## Local simulator and score surrogate

The implementation is
[`simulator.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/simulator.py) and
[`scoring.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/scoring.py). For each
independent 13-week material window, let demand be $d_t$, actual supplier
capacity $K_{ct}$, lead time $\ell_{ct}$, and landed cost $L_{ct}$.
Starting stock is the sum of the first five weeks of demand and transit is
empty. Filled orders, arrivals, served demand, and end stock obey

$$
I_{-1}=\sum_{t=0}^{4}d_t,\quad
f_{ct}=\min(Q_t s_{ct},K_{ct}),\quad
a_\tau=\sum_{c,t:\,t+\ell_{ct}=\tau}f_{ct},\quad
v_t=\min(d_t,I_{t-1}+a_t),\quad
I_t=I_{t-1}+a_t-v_t.
$$

With local average landed cost $A=\operatorname{mean}_{c,t}L_{ct}$, the
material/window cost is

$$
C=\sum_{ct}f_{ct}L_{ct}+0.02A\sum_t I_t
+5A\sum_t(d_t-v_t)
-0.70A\left(I_{12}+\sum_{ct:\,t+\ell_{ct}\ge13}f_{ct}\right).
$$

The meaning of average landed cost and charging holding on end-of-week stock
are **local assumptions**, not proven hidden-simulator internals. The
`sum(first five demands)` convention scored 89.45 on released public data;
an otherwise matched `5 × mean demand` alternative scored 84.27. A hindsight
linear program with perfect released capacity, lead time, and cost supplies
the local lower-bound cost $C_*$.

The local score, before multiplication by 100, is

$$
S/100=0.30P+0.40F+0.30R,
$$
$$
P=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\left[
0.6\left(1-\frac{B}{B_0}\right)
+0.4\left(1-\frac{M}{M_0}\right)\right],\qquad
F=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\frac{C_0-C}{C_0-C_*},
$$
$$
R=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\left[
0.7\frac{r-r_0}{1-r_0}+0.3D\right].
$$

Here $B=\operatorname{mean}(p-y)^2$ is Brier loss,
$M=\operatorname{mean}|\widehat P-P^{actual}|/P^{actual}$ is price MAPE,
and subscript 0 denotes the naive baseline: historical material disruption
rate, last benchmark price, demand-sized orders, and 100% from the largest
normal-capacity supplier. The fill rate $r$ pools demand during disrupted
weeks plus four recovery weeks. The code looks back before the window for a
recent disruption. If there are no shock/recovery demand weeks or $r_0=1$,
the local fill skill is zero. The inferred local diversification measure is
the order-weighted normalized Gini–Simpson index,

$$
D=\frac{\sum_{mt}Q_{mt}\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
\left[\frac{1-\sum_c s_{mct}^{2}}{1-1/3}\right]}
{\sum_{mt}Q_{mt}}.
$$

Order weighting was fitted to public score comparisons, not established from
an official scorer. Public Kaggle results, local historical replays, and
synthetic private scenarios are separate evidence classes throughout this
report.

## Forecasting algorithms

The selected Phase 2 implementation is in
[`sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain).
The older scored files must be reproduced from their own frozen snapshots,
since similar algorithm names do not imply identical defaults or fitted data.

### Supplier onset: gradient-boosted classification

The supplier hit label in
[`labels.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/labels.py) uses observed
capacity below 85% of normal, lead time above baseline, or tariff above
baseline. Training
rows are `(origin, supplier, horizon)` with target
$y_{c,o+h}=\mathbf1\{\text{supplier hit at }o+h\}$, only when the target
week has been released and $1\le h\le26$. An onset classifier is trained
only on origins at which that supplier was **not already hit**. The chosen
[`HistGradientBoostingClassifier`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/models/disruption.py)
uses depth 3, learning rate 0.05, 300 iterations, minimum leaf size 200,
L2 regularization 1, seed 0, and `news+contagion` features. These include
news stage, story age and signal counts, called-off/recovery indicators, and
recent country/lane hit shares. The same module retains standardized logistic
regression with horizon-decayed features and alternate feature-set ablations;
selected strategies use the boosted model.

### Ongoing hits: hierarchical lognormal duration

For a supplier hit at the origin,
[`duration.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/models/duration.py)
models remaining duration rather than a new onset. For shock type $k$,
$\log D_e\sim N(\mu_k,\sigma^2)$. The negative log likelihood adds

$$
\sum_k\frac{(\mu_k-\mu_{pool})^2}{2(0.7)^2}
+\frac{(\mu_{pool}-\log8)^2}{2}
$$

to weighted event likelihood. Ended integer-length runs $d$ contribute
$F_k(d+0.5)-F_k(d-0.5)$; ongoing runs contribute survival beyond
$d-0.5$. Suppliers in the same start/type event have reciprocal group-size
weights, so a shared event counts once. The main fit requires
$\sigma\ge0.6$ and predicts

$$
P(\text{still hit after }h\mid\text{already hit for }a)
=\frac{S_k(a+h-0.5)}{S_k(a-0.5)}.
$$

### News-script conversion and market check

[`scripts.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/models/scripts.py) groups
headlines into type/entity stories: a lane for strikes/closures, a country
for sanctions, and country/material for other types. For resolved or stale
stories reaching stage $j$, the estimated probability of a real event is

$$
\pi_{pool,j}=\frac{n^{real}_{pool,j}+1}{n_{pool,j}+2},\qquad
q_{kj}=\frac{n^{real}_{kj}+2\pi_{pool,j}}{n_{kj}+2}.
$$

Empirical stage-to-onset leads are smoothed by ±1 week for planners and ±3
weeks for market-checked forecast columns: the observed lead gets weight 1
and each offset week gets weight 0.5. The resulting mass is conditioned on the fact that
the event has not started yet. A stage gate estimates the skip rate
$(n_{skip}+1)/(n_{real}+2)$ and moves most next-week onset mass one week
later until the last pre-onset stage is reached. That stage is escalating for
closure and imminent for ban, strike, sanctions, tariff, and shortage. With
onset PMF $g_{kj}(u)$ and duration survival $S_k$,

$$
p^{script}_{c,o+h}=q_{kj}\sum_{u=1}^{h}g_{kj}(u)S_k(h-u+1),\qquad
p^{supply}_{c,o+h}=\max(p^{GBM}_{c,o+h},p^{script}_{c,o+h})
$$

for a previously unhit supplier with an open story. The optional market check
confirms a supplier story when futures-basis z-score is at least 3, or at
least 2 twice consecutively; confirmation raises conversion to at least
0.95. A lane story is confirmed by congestion at least 50 or, for a closure,
tension at least 47 with a jump of at least 10. Confirmation puts 0.9 of the
lane onset mass next week. For a confirmed supplier basis ramp, the onset PMF
is reweighted by

$$
g'_h\ \propto\ g_h\exp\left[-\frac12
\left(\frac{\log(b/peak)-\log\rho_h}{0.4}\right)^2\right],
$$

where $b$ is the current basis and $\rho_h$ the fitted fraction of its
pre-onset peak $h$ weeks before onset. The PMF is renormalized to preserve
its original total mass. Absence of an expected signal scales conversion
odds by an empirically smoothed miss probability. The unannounced basis-ramp
guard is disabled. Market-checked probabilities are used for submitted
forecast columns; the selected planners retain the ordinary supplier
probabilities because feeding the market checks into orders lost financial
score in historical tests.

### Tariff separation and material probability

The Phase 2 [tariff fix](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/models/disruption.py)
places announced-tariff odds in `p_tariff` rather than the supply hit field.
Its effective week follows the announcement and its duration is fitted from
ended tariff events using a lognormal with $\sigma\ge0.3$. It enters
forecast probability and future landed cost, but **not** capacity/lead shock
scenarios. This fixes the earlier KES semiconductor/aluminum tariff being
treated as a certain MERROW closure. Assuming independent suppliers, the
material probability with tariff is

$$
p^{raw}_{m,t}=1-\prod_{c\in C_m}(1-p^{supply}_{c,t})
(1-p^{tariff}_{c,t}).
$$

The supplier planner drops the tariff factor. The submitted material value
also shrinks toward released historical material hit rate $\bar y_m$:

$$
\widehat p_{m,o+h}=(1-\alpha_h)p^{raw}_{m,o+h}+\alpha_h\bar y_m,\qquad
\alpha_h=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}[0.10+0.01(h-1)].
$$

An explicit announced-tariff probability provides a floor after blending.

### Benchmark-price forecast and landed-cost path

The [`price.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/models/price.py) model
separates a slow normal price level from event uplift. Let (z_{m,t}=\log
P_{m,t}), and let $\bar z_{m,o}$ be its trailing 78-week mean (at least
26 observations). For each horizon $h\le26$, quiet released origin/target
pairs fit the reversion fraction

$$
k_h=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\left[
-\frac{\sum_i(z_{i,o}-\bar z_{i,o})(z_{i,o+h}-z_{i,o})}
{\sum_i(z_{i,o}-\bar z_{i,o})^2}\right].
$$

The forecast applies half that reversion. For each shock type $k$, past
events estimate log uplift as the difference between mean log benchmark price
during the event and the mean of weeks onset−6 through onset−3, with one
pseudo-event of zero uplift:

$$
u_k=\max\left(0,\frac{\sum_{e\in k}
[\bar z_{e,during}-\bar z_{e,pre}]}{n_k+1}\right).
$$

An unstarted shock contributes only when supplier hit probability exceeds
0.5. An ongoing shock remains in the price while its survival probability is
at least 0.2. The largest eligible supplier uplift is used per material.
If $u_o$ is uplift already embedded in the last observed price and $u_t$
is predicted uplift, then

$$
\widehat P_{m,o+h}=\exp\left[z_{m,o}-u_o+u_{m,o+h}
-0.5k_h\left((z_{m,o}-\bar z_{m,o})-u_o\right)\right].
$$

The separate
[`cost_path.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/models/cost_path.py)
models landed purchase cost as
$L_{ct}=spot_{ct}(1+tariff_{ct})+freight_{ct}$. Source shocks
(shortage, ban, reversal, sanctions) induce a material-wide spot step.
Past steps use half the onset jump minus the first post-event drop, weight a
shared event once, and shrink type estimates toward the source-shock family
mean by 0.25 pseudo-events. Announced tariff changes apply from their known
effective week. Freight multipliers for lane strikes/closures are supported,
but `path_freight=False` in the selected lean LP because its historical test
worsened financial results. Benchmark price and landed cost have distinct
training targets and are not interchangeable.

## Purchasing algorithms

### Fixed rules and early forecasts

[`pipeline.py`](../pipeline.py) implements the first four scored profiles.
`state_decay` forecasts
$p_{m,o+h}=\bar y_m+(y_{m,o}-\bar y_m)0.8^h$. `news_taper`
adds $(1-p)0.6w_hW_{m,o}0.95^h$, with $w_h=1$ through 13 weeks
and 0.25 beyond; warning strength decays at 0.85 per week. `price_blend`
pulls last price 25% toward the trailing four-week mean.
`horizon_mean13` pulls it 25% toward the trailing 13-week mean by horizon
13; `horizon_mean52` pulls it 50% toward the trailing 52-week mean.
`primary` buys $1.2d_t$ in the first eight weeks and zero for the last
five, with equal thirds across valid suppliers. `independent` and
`long_memory` use `news_risk` orders:
$\operatorname{round}[d_t(1.05+0.7p_{m,o+(t-o+3)})]$ for the first eight
weeks. `lead_aware` adds `late_fast`: after the first six weeks, it blends
equal shares with 55/30/15 allocated by recent lead/cost rank. The
`policy_v1` snapshot introduced the later forecast models and a rule-based
ordering policy; 50.44 isolates its forecast columns on the 44.95 ordering
policy.

### Insured rule planner

The [`policy.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/policy.py) heuristic
projects expected stock. At demand week $\tau$, it targets

$$
stock^{target}_\tau=d_\tau\left[1+b_0+b_r p_{m\tau}
+b_{mid}\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
\left(\frac{12-\tau}{\tau_{taper}}\right)\right].
$$

Among supplier/order-week pairs expected to arrive at $\tau$, it uses

$$
w_c\propto\exp[-\theta(L_c/L_{min}-1)](1-p_c)^\gamma,\qquad
\widetilde w_c=(1-\beta)w_c+\beta/|C_m|,\quad
\beta=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}(b_d+b_{dr}p_{m\tau}).
$$

With hit severity $r_c$, expected fill fraction is
$\phi_c=1-p_c(1-r_c)$. A need slice $n_c$ becomes requested units
$q_c=n_c[1+b_o(1/\phi_c-1)]$, capped by normal capacity. Expected
delivery is $(1-p_c)\min(q_c,K_c)+p_c\min(q_c,K_cr_c)$.
The selected `insured_hedged_mktcol` parameters are

$$
b_0=0.25,\ b_r=4,\ \theta=10,\ \gamma=4,\ b_d=0,\
b_{dr}=2.5,\ b_o=0.333,\ b_{mid}=2.251,\ \tau_{taper}=2.167.
$$
The earlier `policy_v1` and `insured_hedged` snapshots use their own
defaults. Scenario severity ratios are ban 0.05, sanctions 0.20,
shortage/reversal 0.50, closure 0.75, strike 0.80, tariff 1.00. Added
lead times are closure 3, strike 4, sanctions 3 weeks. These are estimated
planning constants, not known future supply.

### Lean stochastic LP (sample-average approximation)

[`policy_lp.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/policy_lp.py) draws 30
scenarios per material/window with seed 0. In each scenario, one
$U_c\sim Uniform(0,1)$ is drawn per supplier, and supplier $c$ is hit
in week $t$ if $p_{ct}>U_c$. This preserves weekly marginal hit
probabilities and tends to make contiguous shock spells. Suppliers on a
common lane share the draw for lane events within the material. The optional
background hazard learns country onset rates from released history with 400
pseudo-supplier-weeks of pooled-rate shrinkage; it is disabled in the chosen
lean policy (`hazard_mult=0`).

Decision $x_{ct}$ is requested supplier units; in scenario $n$,
$f_{nct}$, $I_{nt}$, and $u_{nt}$ are filled units, end stock, and unmet
demand. The sample-average objective is

$$
\min_x\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[
\sum_{ct}f_{nct}L_{nct}+0.02A_n\sum_tI_{nt}
+5A_n\sum_tu_{nt}
-0.70A_n(I_{n,12}+transit_n)\right].
$$

Inventory conservation applies in each scenario, along with
$0\le f_{nct}\le x_{ct}$,
$f_{nct}\le K^{normal}_{ct}r_{nct}$,
$0\le u_{nt}\le d_t$, and
$0\le x_{ct}\le0.96K^{normal}_{ct}$. For unhit scenario weeks,
$f_{nct}=x_{ct}$. SciPy `linprog(method="highs")` solves the sparse LP.
`price_path=True` makes $L_{nct}$ and its mean $A_n$ scenario specific,
allowing pre-buying before a forecast spot or tariff jump. Optional
`fill_kappa` adds extra unmet cost on shock/recovery weeks when the primary
supplier is hit, but is zero in the selected files.

The 60.66 `lean_hedged` file uses the earlier lean LP plus a calm forecast
hedge. The 67.74 `lean_split` adds a near-cost constraint: if a noncheapest
supplier is within 2.5% of the cheapest average planning cost, it receives
at least 30% of each positive order. The chosen Phase 2
`lean_pxlp_mkt_div` adds scenario price paths and minimum per-supplier
shares for steel 20%, copper 10%, aluminum 10%. Its LP settings include
`n_scenarios=30`, `min_share=0`, `hazard_mult=0`, `cap_derate=0.96`,
`price_path=True`, `path_freight=False`, `fill_kappa=0`. Zero-order rows
receive equal shares among valid suppliers, preserving the CSV contract.

### Forecast hedges and final-pair conditioning

[`strategies.py`](sk4_phase2/src/dsdg_supply_chain/strategies.py) makes two
complementary policies. For materials without a live nonoverdue news story,
`calm` halves disruption probabilities when $h>2$; `shock` changes them
when $h>4$ to

$$
p'_{m,o+h}=p_{m,o+h}+0.5\,
\operatorname{clip}_{[0,2]}\left(\frac{\bar y_m}
{\operatorname{mean}_j\bar y_j}\right)(1-p_{m,o+h}),
$$

and ramps predicted benchmark prices by $1+0.05h/13$ to week 13. Live
stories are exempt from generic probability hedges; ongoing shocks are not.
`mktcol` strategies replace submitted forecast columns with the
market-checked forecast while preserving orders and shares from the ordinary
planner probabilities. The two parent policies are `lean_pxlp_mkt_div`
(cheaper, 947,590 private units) and `insured_hedged_mktcol` (buffered,
1,073,023 private units).

The final transform
[`private_closure_conditional.py`](../experiments/private_closure_conditional.py)
reruns the week-221 market-checked forecast three ways: ordinary closure
conversion $p_b$, conversion forced to zero $p_0$, and forced to one
$p_1$. At $\alpha=1$, only private disruption probabilities change:

$$
p^{lean}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}[p^{parent}_{lean}
-\alpha\max(0,p_b-p_0)],\qquad
p^{insured}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}[p^{parent}_{insured}
+\alpha\max(0,p_1-p_b)].
$$

This keeps GBM and other-risk probability in the no-closure branch. Exactly
40 lean and 67 insured private probability cells change; all public cells and
all private price, order, and share cells remain as in their respective SK4
parents. Their different orders, not just probabilities, justify keeping two
files for final private selection.

## Released-public optimization algorithms and score lineage

The Phase 1 scored sequence uses `primary` (44.95), `lead_aware` (45.80),
`long_memory` (46.21), `independent` (46.22), `insured_hedged` (48.55),
forecast-only hybrid (50.44), `policy_v1` (54.10), `lean_hedged` (60.66),
`dual_hazard_three_lane` (66.98), `lithium_share_only` (67.26), and
`lean_split` (67.74). The exact source for every file is in the table above.
The lithium tests changed only released public rows from 67.74 and scored
lower. The 50.44 hybrid held 44.95 orders/shares fixed, isolating forecast
gains; the 54.10 full file then changed orders/shares.

For Phase 2, the
[`phase2_public_oracle.py`](../experiments/phase2_public_oracle.py)
experiment fills released public probabilities and prices with observed
answers and solves a perfect-information HiGHS inventory LP for public
orders. It retains an older week-221 `lean_split` forecast/policy for all
private rows. The `sum5` starting-stock convention scored 89.45; the
otherwise matched `5mean_supweek` convention scored 84.27. Neither result
validates the private plan. The later 91.36, 92.31, and 92.62 files leave
**all private rows and all forecast columns identical** to 89.45.

The 91.36/92.31
[`public_diversification_qp.py`](../experiments/public_diversification_qp.py)
fixes public total orders $Q_t$ and solves a convex quadratic program
over supplier shares:

$$
\min_s\ \frac{40}{G}\sum_{tc}Q_t s_{ct}
[L_{ct}+0.02A\max(13-t-\ell_{ct},0)-0.70A]
+\lambda\frac{9}{130(2/3)}\sum_{tc}s_{ct}^2,
$$

where $G=5{,}728{,}480.540944307-4{,}256{,}386.797304769$ is a
local baseline-to-hindsight cost gap and $\lambda=0.5$ for 91.36,
$\lambda=1.5$ for 92.31. Share sums, supplier capacity, and
cumulative deliveries constrain the solution. SLSQP solves it; the script
then repairs tiny stockouts caused by integer order and share serialization.
The 92.62
[`public_joint_order_search.py`](../experiments/public_joint_order_search.py)
instead optimizes supplier units $x_{ct}$, so $Q_t=\sum_cx_{ct}$ can
change, with objective

$$
\min_x\ \frac{40}{G}\sum_{tc}x_{ct}
[L_{ct}+0.02A\max(13-t-\ell_{ct},0)-0.70A]
+1.5\frac{9}{130(2/3)}\sum_t\sum_c
\left(\frac{x_{ct}}{\sum_jx_{jt}}\right)^2.
$$

Its active order weeks come from 92.31; capacity and cumulative-arrival
constraints apply. This is a nonlinear SLSQP program because shares are
ratios. The exact scored 92.62 CSV is preserved: current source/runtime
regeneration differs on two public orders by at most two units and some
public shares by at most 0.001, while forecasts and all private rows match.
The public-only improvement should not be used to select a private policy.

## Evidence and limitations for the selected final pair

The SK4 parent files and the selected conditional files each scored **89.45
publicly**, as confirmed by the user. Their common released-public rows
explain the tie. The canonical [`submission89.45.csv`](../submissions/submission89.45.csv)
has the same public score but a different, older private plan, so it is not
one of these final files. The final pair's exact hashes, source strategies,
parent artifacts, and generation command are in the
[`candidate artifact record`](../submissions/candidates/README.md).

In a documented 48-world local private scenario study, the original SK4
pair's mean **best-of-two** score was 81.80 and the selected conditional
pair's was 82.59. The study assumed a 0.4 closure probability, the announced
tariff, and two warned bans; it omitted unwarned shocks. The best-of-two
score is computed separately in each scenario before averaging. Across ten
historical origin cutoffs with a live closure warning, the full pair's local
score improved in four, fell 0.03 in one, and was unchanged in five. Those
windows overlap and include only one historical advance-warning MERROW
closure. The supporting reports are
[`private_closure_scenario_summary.md`](../reports/private_closure_scenario_summary.md),
[`private_closure_backtest.csv`](../reports/private_closure_backtest.csv),
and [`private_closure_pair_backtest.csv`](../reports/private_closure_pair_backtest.csv).
A stronger closure-mixture inversion looked better in the synthetic scenarios
but lost 3.13 locally on a false-warning historical window; the selected
transform retains the independent-risk floor. No private leaderboard score
has been observed for the final files.

Other implemented ablations include logistic versus gradient boosting,
alternate feature sets, background-hazard multipliers, capacity derating,
extra shock-fill penalties, freight price paths, entropy versus HHI public
objectives, ridge regressions, CVaR LPs, event-based scenario planning, and
hindsight imitation. Their research scripts, reports, and rejection evidence
are mapped in the [root inventory](../README.md); they are not additional
graded or final artifacts. The selected pipeline is CPU based: scikit-learn
boosting, SciPy SLSQP, and small HiGHS LPs. A GPU could speed larger future
searches but would not change the information available at week 221.

python3 methods/check_provenance.py --python /path/to/python-with-dependencies
python3 methods/check_provenance.py --final-only --python /path/to/python-with-dependencies
python3 tools/code_inventory.py

from the frozen phase inputs and their exact code snapshots, checking 15
byte for byte and the bounded public numerical drift of 92.62. It also
regenerates the two selected final files into a temporary directory and
compares every byte with the saved artifacts. It does not overwrite scored
or final CSVs. `--final-only` checks just that pair. `code_inventory.py`
checks that the root README maps all
Python files. `submission_audit.py` validates 260 IDs, numeric ranges,
eligible country shares, share sums, and full/public duplicate groups.
